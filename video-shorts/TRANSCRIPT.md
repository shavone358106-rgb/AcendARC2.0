## One job: what a short is for

A short has one job: get the right person to raise their hand. Not go viral. Not teach everything.

So every short gives a real payoff in about 30 seconds, leaves one question open, then makes one ask. The last line is always the same shape:

> **CTA LINE:** If you want [the thing], comment [WORD].

That comment triggers an automatic DM with the link. The viewer gets what they asked for. You get a comment (which helps the short spread) and a conversation you can keep going.

**One topic, one short.** My first version cut three shorts from every long video. Now it's one topic, one short. Each one gets its own hook, its own keyword and its own day. A long video still feeds the system, but it turns into several topics posted on different days, never a batch dropped at once.

**The loop:** one topic, one short, one comment, one DM, one warm lead.

1. **Topic.** A proven idea, tagged with a lane.
2. **1 short.** One hook, one ask, about 30 seconds.
3. **Comment.** Viewer types your keyword.
4. **DM.** They get the link automatically.
5. **Warm lead.** A guide, a video, or your community.

**What the system produces, per topic:**

- **1 vertical short:** about 30 to 34 seconds, 9:16, with your face, captions, graphics and music.
- **One keyword:** a short, sayable word tied to the topic. Checked so it never collides with your other DM automations.
- **Captions per platform:** post text written for each app, because not every app can answer a comment.
- **A cover and a QA report:** one cover for Instagram and YouTube. What it checked, what it fixed, and frames for you to look at.

## Three lanes: what each comment gets

Where a topic comes from decides what the viewer gets when they comment. Every topic gets a lane before anything is written.

- **Top (about 60% of posts).** Source: other creators' outlier videos, GitHub repos taking off, new tool launches. The ask: comment a keyword. They get: a free guide page on your site: the setup, screenshots, prompts. Your community is never pitched in the short.
- **Middle (about 20%).** Source: your own long videos that haven't been cut into shorts yet. The ask: comment a keyword. They get: the link to the full video.
- **Bottom (about 20%).** Source: one lesson from inside your paid community. The ask: comment one fixed word, every time. Mine is CLUB. They get: your community. Say its name out loud ("The full lesson is inside [your community]") and show the real classroom on that line.

**Why split it.** Top-lane shorts bring in strangers with something free and useful. Middle-lane shorts turn them into viewers of your long videos. Bottom-lane shorts show people who already know you what's inside your community.

Each short still makes one ask. The second step lives in the DM: every guide DM also carries one button to the community.

**Finding topics worth a short:**

- **Outliers, not big numbers.** A video counts when it beat its creator's own median views by a wide margin. Big accounts get big numbers on everything.
- **Fresh.** Trend picks come from the last 7 days.
- **Fits what you teach.** Skip hot topics outside your lane.
- **Not seen, not made.** Skip what three competitors already covered, and anything you already posted.

> **PITFALL:** Picking topics by gut every morning. Have Claude pull the numbers on a schedule and hand you two or three topic cards a day: the source, its real numbers, the lane, a few proven hook options and a keyword idea. You pick.

## The ten stages, from topic to scheduled short

Build these one at a time. Get each one working by hand in a normal Claude Code chat first. Once it works three times in a row, fold it into the skill.

## The pieces to think about

### A. Steal hooks. Don't write them.

This rule changed my results more than any other. Hooks I wrote from scratch were weak. So every hook now starts from someone else's winning opening, taken word for word, with only the slots swapped: the tool, the topic, the number, the audience.

- **Only real winners:** a hook qualifies when its video got at least 3x its creator's median views. Under that, it's noise.
- **Keep the words:** keep the wording and the word order. My bar: at least 70% of the original words survive. If it needs more changes, pick a different winner.
- **It must read cold:** a stranger with zero context gets it on the first listen. No inside terms, no jargon, no "it" or "this" with nothing to point at.
- **Real numbers only:** use your own proof numbers when you have them. Never invent one, never round one up.
- **Write down the source:** keep the link and the views next to every hook, so you can see which patterns keep winning.

> **PITFALL:** Keeping the pattern and filling it with nonsense. One of my hooks kept a proven shape but swapped in a detail from inside a lesson. Nobody outside the lesson could follow it. The shape is not enough.

**Prompt 1: Steal a proven hook** (Write)

```
Here is a proven hook, word for word: "[WINNING HOOK]"
Source: [LINK], [VIEWS] views. The creator's median is [MEDIAN VIEWS].
My topic: [TOPIC]. My real numbers: [NUMBERS + WHERE EACH ONE IS FROM].
First check the ratio. If views are under 3x the median, stop and tell me to
pick a different hook. Adapt it to my topic. Keep every other word and the word
order. Swap only the slots: the tool, the topic, the number, the audience.
Give me 3 versions. For each, show the share of original words you kept (aim
for 70% or more) and what you swapped.
Then read each one as a stranger who has never heard of me. Flag any word that
won't make sense cold: inside terms, jargon, "it" or "this" with nothing to
point at. Never add a number I didn't give you.
```

### B. Lock the script before the avatar.

Every word you change after the voice is made costs a new render. My first Club lesson short took 8 avatar runs because the script kept changing. Now the script is locked first, and the goal is one render per short.

- **Hook (line 1):** the stolen hook, slots swapped.
- **The problem (line 2):** the viewer's real problem, in plain words.
- **The steps:** say the order out loud: "First..." then "Then..."
- **The twist:** sounds like talking: "But here's the trick if you want to [bigger outcome]. Don't [obvious move]. Instead, [better move]."
- **A list of 3:** three things a graphic can show. "But here's the best part" goes before the best one, not the first.
- **Pre-CTA + CTA:** what only the link gives you. Then the CTA line for the lane, word for word.

> **PITFALL:** Numbers the voice can't say. AI voices read "$5.70" digit by digit. In the spoken script, write money as words ("five dollars and seventy cents"). Captions and graphics keep the numerals.

**Prompt 2: Lock the script with a cold read** (Check, subagent)

```
Here's my short script: [SCRIPT]. The lane is [TOP / MIDDLE / BOTTOM] and the
last line must be: "[CTA LINE]".
Use a subagent that has never seen this project to check it before I record
anything:
1) Read every line as a stranger. Flag any line that isn't clear on its own,
and offer a plainer one.
2) Line 2 names the viewer's real problem in plain words.
3) Steps say "First" and "Then" out loud.
4) Every line ends on a noun or number a graphic can show.
5) 105 to 125 words. No hype word without a number next to it.
6) Money is written as words for the voice ("five dollars and seventy cents").
Numerals only in captions and graphics.
7) Every number has a source I gave you. Cut any that don't.
Return the fixed script and a list of what changed and why. Once I say
"locked", don't change a word.
```

### C. A look that's yours.

Pick one style and lock it. Write it down as a short style sheet: two or three colors, one or two fonts, how things move, what a caption looks like. Then every short starts from that sheet, not from a blank page.

The four words I build to: simple, bold, big, clean. Every graphic has to read in about one second for someone who has never seen you. Premium comes from light, depth and smooth motion. It never comes from adding more stuff. If a polish pass makes a frame slower to read, undo it.

The title on screen at frame 0 is not the spoken hook. It's 3 to 8 words, leads with what the viewer gets, and poses the question the short answers. It also becomes your cover.

*[Image: "My locked look: dark ground, one accent color, big type." — frame titled "FROM THE MATH UP" with a step ladder "01 MATH / 02 DEEP LEARNING", caption "the", face card at bottom.]*

> **PITFALL:** Changing the look every video. Your viewers learn your style. Change it on purpose, once, after you test a new one side by side.

**Prompt 3: Lock my look** (Style sheet)

```
Help me write a one-page style sheet for my short videos. My vibe is [3 WORDS,
e.g. dark, bold, techy]. Here are 2 or 3 creators or screenshots I like: [LINKS
OR IMAGES]. Give me: a dark look and a light look, one accent color for each,
one or two fonts, how captions look, how things move in and out, and 5 things I
should never do. Rule for every choice: a stranger reads any frame in about one
second. Keep it simple, bold, big and clean. Save it as [FOLDER]/STYLE.md.
```

### D. Captions.

One or two words at a time, cut exactly on the spoken word. Big and bold, so they read on a phone in half a second. Never on top of a graphic. Hide them when a graphic already shows the same words.

> **PITFALL:** Captions that drift. Get word-level timestamps from the audio and snap every caption to them. Being off by a quarter second feels broken.

### E. Layout and safe zones.

Instagram covers parts of the video with its own buttons and text. Keep anything people need to read out of those spots:

- **Top strip:** keep it clear of text. The app puts its own UI there.
- **Bottom fifth:** covered by your caption and username. No important text here.
- **Right edge, lower half:** the like, comment and share buttons live here. Keep text in from the edge.

Pick one home layout and use it most of the time. Mine: the graphic gets the top two thirds, one caption line, and my face as a cutout in a small card at the bottom. Center everything. A graphic hugging the left side with empty space on the right looks unfinished.

### F. Pacing.

Cut when the idea changes. Hang while it builds. When one idea runs for a few seconds, keep one graphic up and add to it as you speak: a new row, a new step, a highlight. Something new should land about every second. The whole graphic changes when the idea changes.

Two exceptions. The hook holds: no cuts until the first sentence ends. And the problem line and the "But here's the trick" line play full frame on your face. Graphics start on the first step.

Every visual change gets a quiet sound: a whoosh on a cut, a tick on a build step. Under it all, one energetic music bed that sits well below your voice.

> **PITFALL:** Cutting on every line. Six fast cuts feel busy and people lose the thread. One graphic that builds in time with your words feels smooth.

### G. Dark and light for pacing.

My shorts run in two tones: a dark look and a light paper look. The designed graphics split fairly evenly between them, roughly 40 to 60% each. They flip in runs, on a hard cut, when the idea changes. Each flip works like a chapter change and gives the eye a fresh start.

The hook and the ending always stay dark, so they feel like home. Real screenshots keep their own tone, even for one beat inside the other run. Never fade between tones, and never flip back and forth every beat.

*[Image: "The dark ending, and a light run in the middle. Numbers always count up." — left: dark frame "Comment [CLUB] for the full lesson"; right: light paper frame with a big "1", caption "from".]*

> **PITFALL:** Mostly one tone. My early shorts were almost all dark and felt flat by the middle. Count the split on every short.

### H. Study creators you admire, frame by frame.

My best rules came from taking apart other people's reels. Download a few, pull a frame every half second, and write down what's on screen and when it changes. How long does the hook hold? How often does something new land? When do they show their face? How do their covers and captions work? You'll find patterns you'd never catch watching at normal speed.

**Prompt 4: Study creators frame by frame** (Research, subagents)

```
I want to learn how these creators build their short videos: [LINK 1], [LINK
2], [LINK 3]. Start one subagent per video, all at the same time. Each one
should: download the video, pull a frame every 0.5 seconds, get the transcript
with timestamps, and write down: how long the hook holds, how often something
new appears on screen, when the face shows, what the captions look like, what
graphic sits under each type of line, and how the cover and the post caption
work.
Each subagent saves notes to [FOLDER]/creator-name.md and reports back in under
150 words. Then combine them into one list of rules I could copy, with the
timestamp that proves each rule.
```

### I. What to show.

For every line, ask what the line is doing. That decides what goes on screen.

- **A claim ("56,000 people starred it"):** the real thing, within 2 seconds. Proof first, then explain.
- **How it works:** a graphic that draws the idea: steps, a flow, a before and after.
- **A number:** the number, huge, counting up. Numbers never just appear.
- **A list:** one visual per spoken item, landing on its word.
- **A prompt, or what Claude does:** the Claude app: the prompt types in on your voice, sends, and Claude answers. Not a terminal. Most beginners use the app.
- **An opinion or payoff:** your face, for 1 to 2 seconds.

**Three tests every visual passes:**

- **The one-second test.** Every screenshot has to read at phone size in about a second. Both sides of any comparison visible and labeled. If it fails, don't use it. Build your own graphic with the real numbers and a small source label.
- **Named means shown.** If you say a prompt, a skill or a tool, it's on screen, even for a second. A tool with a logo gets its real logo. Never redraw one.
- **Crop to the word.** If you say one number, show that number big, not the whole page it lives on.

Real footage and screenshots are the proof, not the whole story. In my shorts they're about a quarter of the beats. Graphics carry the rest.

### J. A graphics library you grow.

Don't design every short from scratch. Build a small library of reusable motion graphics in your style: a big number, a list that builds, a step ladder, a before/after, a prompt in the app, your CTA ending. Start with six. Add one every time a script needs something the library can't draw.

Then write a one-page "which graphic when" guide. It maps the kind of line to the graphic. That page is what lets Claude make good picks on its own.

*[Image: "Same graphic, two passes. Left: first build. Right: after a polish pass. Bigger number, clear label, more light." — "0.1s TIME TO FIRST TOKEN — faster than you can blink".]*

> **PITFALL:** A library with no guide. Claude will pick the same two graphics every time, or a random one. The guide is where your taste lives.

**Prompt 5: Build a graphics library and its guide** (Build, subagents)

```
Using [FOLDER]/STYLE.md, build a small library of reusable motion graphics for
9:16 video in [HyperFrames / Remotion / After Effects]. Start with 6: a big
number that counts up, a list that adds one row per spoken word, a step ladder,
a before/after, a prompt typing into the Claude app and getting an answer, and
my ending card with a comment keyword. One subagent per graphic. Each takes
text and timing as inputs, renders a 3-second test and saves a still.
Then write a one-page guide: which graphic for which kind of line (claim,
number, list, steps, prompt, opinion, hook, ending), when to cut and when to
keep building, a "never do" list, and the lines we have no graphic for yet.
Save it as [FOLDER]/GRAPHICS.md.
```

### K. QA by actually watching.

A render that "passed" can still be bad. Have Claude pull frames from the finished video, a few per second, and look at every one at a size you can read. Text cut off, an empty frame, a screenshot nobody can read, a caption on top of a graphic, a beat too short to read. List the problems, fix them, render again, until every frame earns its place.

Add a few automatic checks too: total length, loudness, "no cut during the hook", and that the face and voice come from the same take.

*[Image: "A QA frame. Is the text readable at phone size?" — frame with "PROMPT" icon, caption "A prompt,", face card.]*

**Prompt 6: QA this render frame by frame** (Check, subagent)

```
Use a subagent to QA [VIDEO FILE]. Pull 3 frames per second and put them on
contact sheets, then open the frames at full size. List each problem with its
timestamp: text cut off or too small on a phone, empty or black frames, a
screenshot you can't read in a second, captions on top of a graphic, something
shown before it's said, beats too short to read, a cut during the hook, text in
the app's safe zones. Also check length ([TARGET] seconds) and loudness.
Return the list, worst first. Then fix them, render again, and repeat until the
list is empty. Save the sheets so I can look too.
```

### L. Dissect the script into visual moments.

Planning one graphic per line misses things, because one line often holds two pictures. "Start at lesson one and grind through all 523" goes from one lesson to all of them. A single static graphic can't show that. A zoom out from one tile to the full grid can.

So before picking any graphics, have Claude split every line into small moments. For each one it writes down: when it's spoken, its job (hook, claim, number, list item, payoff), the nouns you can draw, and the verb. The verb picks the motion. "Turns into" is a morph. "Pick one" is a pick. "Send it to" shows the thing being sent. "You don't" is a cut to your face.

**Example, one moment at a time:**

- **"This free GitHub repo"** (hook): the repo name and logo land under the title.
- **"is a 342-hour AI course"** (number): 342 counts up on the same board. No cut.
- **"Start at lesson one"** (setup): close up on a single lesson tile.
- **"and grind through all 523"** (one to all): zoom out on "all" to reveal the full grid.
- **"You don't."** (turn): hard cut to my face for under a second.

> **PITFALL:** Giving a tiny moment its own cut. Anything spoken in under about half a second should be a step on the graphic already on screen, never a new shot.

*[Image: "Part of my graphics library: hubs, loops, lists and flows. Each one builds in time with the words." — six frames: Claude Code hub (Plugins, Skills, MCP, Subagents, CLAUDE.md, Hooks); "What Claude Code Can Actually Do" list; "The agent loop" (Read, Think, Act, Check); "A self-improving skill" loop (Analyze, Test, Edit, Generate, Learn); "Ralph loop" (Prompt, Run, Review); "What goes in CLAUDE.md" (Commands, Style rules, Never do).]*

**Prompt 7: Dissect a script into visual moments** (Plan, subagent)

```
Take the locked script at [SCRIPT FILE] and the word timestamps from [AUDIO OR
AVATAR FILE]. Use a subagent to split every sentence into small visual moments.
For each: start and end time, its job (hook, claim, number, list item,
payoff...), the nouns you can draw, and the verb. The verb picks the motion
(turns into = morph, pick = pick, one to all = zoom out, send to = show it
being sent). Then pick what to show from [FOLDER]/GRAPHICS.md: the real thing,
a graphic, or my face. Rules: the hook has no cuts; anything under half a
second is a step on the current graphic; claims show proof within 2 seconds;
every screenshot must read in one second at phone size, or we build our own
graphic instead. Show me the plan as a table before building.
```

## After you post: comments, captions, covers

The short is half the system. The other half turns a comment into a follow, a click and a conversation.

### A. Comment to DM.

The keyword fires an automatic DM with the link. Three details made mine work better:

- **A trigger word nobody else uses.** Before you write the script, check the word against every DM automation you run, in every tool, every keyword you've used before, and a list of common words people type anyway. If two reels share a word, one can fire the other's DM. Never reuse a retired word.
- **A follow gate on Instagram.** The DM asks them to follow first, then sends the link. People who already follow skip that step. Reels reach mostly people who don't follow you yet, so turning a comment into a follow is worth a small drop in delivery.
- **A public reply.** Under every keyword comment, a short "sent, check your DMs" reply. The next viewer sees the keyword works.

The DM also carries a second button to your community, named for what people get, in their words.

**Prompt 8: Check a trigger word** (Check)

```
I want [WORD] as the comment keyword for a short about [TOPIC]. Check it's free
before I write the script. Look in my keyword list at [FILE], every comment-to-
DM automation in [MY DM TOOLS], and my last [NUMBER] post captions. Reject it
if it's used anywhere, if it's a common word people type anyway (yes, love,
info, link), or if it's hard to spell after hearing it once. If it fails,
suggest 5 short, fun, sayable words tied to the topic and check those too.
When one passes, add it to my list as reserved, with the topic and today's
date. Never reuse a word that was retired.
```

### B. Captions per platform.

Only some apps can answer a comment, so each app gets its own caption.

- **Instagram, Facebook:** line 1 is the offer: Comment "WORD" to get [the thing] + [the result]. A blank line, then the hook and the script as short one-line paragraphs.
- **TikTok, YouTube, X, LinkedIn:** no comment promise, because nothing answers it there. Same hook and body, then the link: the guide, the video, or your community.
- **Hashtags:** five at most. Instagram rejects more.

> **PITFALL:** Pasting the Instagram caption everywhere. On TikTok, "comment WORD" gets comments nobody answers, and those people never come back.

**Prompt 9: Captions for every platform** (Write)

```
Write post captions for my short. Script: [SCRIPT]. The comment gets: [THE
THING]. Keyword: [WORD]. Link: [LINK].
Instagram and Facebook: line 1 is exactly
Comment "[WORD]" to get [the thing] + [the result].
Then a blank line, then the hook and the script as short one-line paragraphs.
TikTok, YouTube, X and LinkedIn: no comment promise, since nothing answers
comments there. Same hook and body, then end with the link. Five hashtags at
most per platform. Plain words, no hype. Save one block per platform in one
file, ready to paste.
```

### C. Covers.

One cover per short. It's the Instagram cover and the YouTube thumbnail. Never a raw frame grab. Two recipes:

- **Idea, lesson or opinion shorts:** built from the short's own title frame. Same look, bigger and bolder, your face as a clean cutout.
- **Tool, repo or launch shorts:** a template: the tool's logo and name across the top, your face in the middle with two app tiles at ear level, a two-line title under your chin.

Keep the logo, face and title inside the middle 3:4 of the frame, because your profile grid crops the top and bottom. Your face is always bright and clear.

*[Image: "Everything important sits inside the grid crop." — cover "Turn Claude Code into a client machine" with Bakery / Dentist / Plumber tiles and the creator's face.]*

**Prompt 10: Cover prompt skeleton** (Image)

```
A 9:16 short-video cover. Image 1 is my face. Image 2 is the [TOOL] logo. Top:
that logo and the name "[TOOL NAME]" in large clean type. Middle: my face,
centered, smiling, wearing [WHAT I WEAR], lit with bright, even studio light
and true skin tones. Two equal app tiles at ear level, one each side, slightly
behind my head: [TOOL 1] and [TOOL 2]. Under my chin, a tight two-line title: "
[LINE 1]" / "[LINE 2]", line 2 on a [ACCENT COLOR] bar. Leave the top and
bottom 13% empty.
```

> **TIP:** For an idea or lesson short, add the short's title frame as image 3 and ask for "the same look, bigger and bolder." Refer to images by number and describe logos by how they look.

### D. Cadence.

One post a day, same morning slot. That's my pace until the system runs clean at that speed. Never use the same music bed as the post the day before or after. A small library of approved beds you like beats making new music for every short.

### E. Reuse the winners in your community.

Two days after a free-guide short goes live, count how many different people commented the keyword. Twice a week, the best one becomes a short first-person post in your community's resources section, linking the tool or the guide.

Each post says who it's for and when to skip it ("if you're busy shipping, bookmark it and keep going"). That honesty is why people trust the next one. Never post the same resource twice.

> **PITFALL:** Linking something that isn't live yet. Check every link right before the post goes up, and skip that slot if one fails.

## How to use subagents

A **subagent** is a helper Claude Code starts for one job. It works in its own fresh chat, then hands back a short report. Your main chat never fills up with its searching and reading.

**Why they matter here.** This system reads a lot: transcripts, video frames, other people's reels, your style notes. Put all of that in one chat and it gets slow and forgetful. Split it up and each helper stays sharp.

**Why they're faster.** Once the script is locked, the voice render, the guide page, the captions and the graphics plan can all run at the same time. Only the final build waits on the voice. Each short gets its own folder, so several shorts can run side by side.

**The pattern:**

- **One helper per job.** Research, build, write or check. Never "do everything."
- **A brief that stands alone.** The helper can't see your chat. Give it the files to read, the goal, the rules, the budget and where to save the result.
- **Ask for a short report back.** What it made, where it saved it, and anything it wasn't sure about.
- **You review.** Read the report, open the files, decide. Then the next step starts.

**Six splits that work:**

- **Topic scout** (research): pulls outliers and fresh repos, returns two or three topic cards.
- **Creator study** (research): one helper per creator. Frames plus transcript, returns their rules.
- **Cold reader** (write): reads the script as a stranger and flags every unclear line.
- **Graphics builders** (build): one helper per new graphic, each tested on its own.
- **Dissector** (plan): turns a script into visual moments and graphic picks.
- **QA reviewer** (check): pulls frames, reads every one, returns a list of defects.

> **PITFALL:** Handing off the thinking. A helper can find, build and check. You still decide what's good. If you skip your review, mistakes pile up quietly.

## Before you post: the checklist

**The hook and script:**

- One topic, one short, one lane. The CTA line matches the lane, word for word.
- The hook comes from a winner at 3x or more, word for word, only the slots swapped.
- A stranger gets every line on the first listen. Every number has a source.
- Money is written as words for the voice. The script was locked before the voice was made.

**The picture:**

- The hook holds with no cuts until the first sentence ends.
- Every claim shows the real thing within 2 seconds. Every screenshot reads in one second, or it became your own graphic.
- Nothing readable sits in the top strip, the bottom fifth or the right-edge buttons.
- Captions are 1 or 2 words and land on the spoken word.
- You watched it on your phone, with sound, start to finish.

**The loop:**

- The keyword passed the check and no other automation uses it.
- You tested the DM from a second account, follow gate included.
- Instagram and Facebook captions open with the comment line. The others carry the link.
- The cover still works after the profile grid crops it.
- You said go. Nothing was posted by the skill alone.
